Chiedozie David Nnam

This is a record of how three principles of discipleship have shaped my growth as a technologist, and data scientist in the course of completing IT 497.

The Three Cornerstone Principles

1. Stewardship — "I am a steward, not an owner."

Mosiah 2:17 · Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28

As a system administrator, I hold admin privileges and access to other people's data. As an AI engineer, I will shape how models are trained and used. This principle reframes both roles: I do not own the systems, data, or capabilities entrusted to me — I am accountable for how I use them. Stewardship is not passive custodianship; it is the active, voluntary exercise of agency in a good cause.

How it shapes my decisions: Before any access decision, deployment, or data export, I ask: Am I being a faithful steward of what belongs to someone else — and ultimately to God? In the Heritage Vault refinement, this question is what drove the separation of public records from restricted ordinance records.

In practice: Separate ZFS datasets for sacred data · file-level encryption for ordinance records · consent checks before deploying any model.

2. Integrity — "Do what is right in the unseen work."

Doctrine and Covenants 64:33–34 · Alma 37:6–7

Much of system administration and AI engineering happens behind the scenes — logs nobody reads, models nobody audits, scripts nobody reviews. This principle holds that integrity is measured in the invisible work. The Lord requires "the heart and a willing mind," and by small and simple things great things are brought to pass — including the small, unseen, consistent choices that build or erode trust.

How it shapes my decisions: I document work no one may ever read. I report my own design errors honestly — including the undersized backup volume I found and disclosed during the Week 4 refinement rather than quietly fixing it.

In practice: Audit logs and MFA on every admin account · documentation as an act of honesty · honest reporting of my own errors.

3. Service — "Technical work is an act of love for my neighbor."

Mosiah 2:17 · Matthew 25:40

Every server I maintain and every AI tool I build ultimately touches a real person — a user whose data I protect, a family whose network I keep running, a student who relies on a system I administer. This principle reframes technical excellence as a form of discipleship: I serve my neighbor by doing my work well.

How it shapes my decisions: It shifts my focus from "does this work?" to "does this serve the people who depend on it?" In my teaching role, it meant designing a curriculum for 200 students with no prior exposure to technology. In my AI work, it means building tools that expand access rather than restrict it.

In practice: Curriculum for 200+ students · open-source tools for nonprofit budgets · building access, not restricting it.

Collected Materials by Theme

Theme 1 — Stewardship

Scripture

Doctrine and Covenants 58:26–28

"Men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will... For the power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves."

Why it is meaningful: This is the anchor of my capstone journey. As an IT professional, I am an "agent unto myself" when I hold admin privileges or build AI models. This verse reminds me that my agency is a gift meant for righteousness — I should proactively use my skills to build secure, ethical systems, not wait to be told.

Scripture

Alma 37:6–7

"By small and simple things are great things brought to pass."

Why it is meaningful: System administration and AI engineering involve invisible, repetitive tasks — patching a server, reviewing logs, cleaning data. This scripture reminds me that the small, unseen details are exactly where great things are built. Integrity in the invisible work matters most.

Personal Experience

The Hardware Maintenance Protocol

When I implemented the hardware maintenance protocol at Pi & Beads Academy, I took ownership of a problem no one had asked me to solve. That proactive stewardship extended equipment lifespan by 40%.

Why it is meaningful: Looking back, I see that as a spiritual milestone: a moment where my technical skills and my desire to be a faithful steward aligned. Stewardship is not what I am told to do; it is what I notice needs doing and do.

Theme 2 — Integrity

General Conference

Elder David A. Bednar — "Gather Together in One All Things in Christ" (2018)

The "rope" analogy: doctrine, principles, and practices woven together into a single, unbreakable strand.

Why it is meaningful: This image has shaped how I see this entire course. My system administration skills and my discipleship are not two separate ropes; they must be woven together. A technically brilliant engineer who lacks integrity is a weak strand.

General Conference

President Russell M. Nelson — "Make Time for the Lord"

A reminder that no matter how busy life becomes, the relationship with the Lord comes first.

Why it is meaningful: As I prepare for a demanding career in AI and system administration, my technical ambitions must never crowd out my spiritual priorities. Time with the Lord is what recharges me for the work ahead — and it is the discipline that keeps the two strands of the rope from fraying apart.

Scripture

3 Nephi 13:19–22

"Lay up for yourselves treasures in heaven... For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also."

Why it is meaningful: This passage was assigned for the Week 5 reflection on keeping my eye single to God's will. It asks me to examine where my treasure actually is — not where I say it is. In a field that rewards visibility and speed, it reminds me that the unseen work is the treasure.

Theme 3 — Service

Scripture

Mosiah 2:17

"When ye are in the service of your fellow beings ye are only in the service of your God."

Why it is meaningful: When I troubleshoot a network outage or design an AI tool, it is easy to focus only on the code and forget the people behind it. This verse reframes my technical work as an act of service. Every secure server I maintain and every clean script I write is a way of serving my neighbor.

Personal Experience

District Leader During My Mission

Leading and mentoring missionaries in the Nigeria Benin City Mission taught me that leadership is about lifting others, not just managing tasks.

Why it is meaningful: That lesson applies directly to IT leadership. Whether I am leading a team project or mentoring a junior technician, I want to lead the way Christ did — with patience, honesty, and a focus on the growth of the people I serve.

Personal Insight

The Internship Data Loss

During my internship, our team sent weekly reports to one member for consolidation. When that member's laptop failed, the consolidated data was lost — we recovered only because each of us had backed up our own copies.

Why it is meaningful: This taught me that service includes foresight. Protecting other people's work is a form of service, and preparation is an act of love. It also shaped my discussion board contribution on data stewardship in the workplace.

Personal Warning Signs

1. Rushing past the ethical question

When I am behind and tell myself "I'll think about the ethics later," I have already begun to diverge. Ethics deferred is ethics avoided.

2. Justifying a shortcut because no one will notice

The moment I measure rightness by whether it will be detected, my integrity has slipped into performance.

3. Separating "work me" from "Sunday me"

When I catch myself thinking my faith is relevant only in church settings, that is the clearest sign my rope is fraying into two strands.

4. Hiding a concern instead of voicing it

Silence under pressure is not neutrality — it is complicity.

Strategies for Realignment

  • A five-minute written check before any deployment or data decision: Who is affected? Who bears the risk? Did they consent?
  • A weekly portfolio entry connecting one technical decision to one principle.
  • Monthly mentor conversation where I report honestly on where I fell short.
  • Returning to the temple when I feel distance growing.

Portfolio Review & Enhancement Plan

Findings — What Is Working

  • Clear thematic structure. Every item maps to Stewardship, Integrity, or Service, giving the portfolio a visible through-line.
  • Diverse material types. Scripture, general conference talks, personal experience, and personal insight.
  • Genuine professional connection. Each entry links to system administration or AI engineering rather than sitting in a spiritual silo.

Findings — Areas for Enhancement (and What Was Done)

  • Chronological depth was thin. Added the mission district-leader experience and the internship data-loss experience to extend the arc beyond this course. Further work: add one family milestone.
  • "Unseen work" needed a concrete example. Added the Heritage Vault refinement as the concrete instance where integrity shaped a real deliverable.
  • No record of near-misses. Added the Near-Miss Log above, with three documented instances.
  • Scripture connections were asserted, not demonstrated. Revised every principle entry to include a "How it shapes my decisions" section tracing the actual effect.

Integration goal: The final portfolio should read as a single coherent narrative of one disciple-engineer growing in both dimensions — not two files stapled together. This page and the Professional Growth Portfolio are deliberately designed to read as one story with two doors.
