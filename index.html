Guiding Principles

Stewardship

I am a steward, not an owner, of what has been entrusted to me. Admin privileges, user data, and AI capabilities are held in trust — I am accountable for how I use them, whether or not anyone is auditing my work.

Integrity

I do what is right, especially in the unseen work. Logs, patches, and model training are acts of honesty, not hidden shortcuts. The work no one sees is the work that matters most.

Service

Technical work is an act of love for my neighbor. Every system I build or maintain serves real people, and I treat technical excellence itself as discipleship.

Project Refinement: Heritage Vault 2.0 Proposal

Original project: In IT 370 (Linux Administration), I wrote a technical implementation proposal for FamilySearch's simulated "Heritage Vault 2.0" modernization. The original draft addressed high-volume access, security, storage, and performance using HAProxy, SELinux, ZFS, and Keepalived — but treated each requirement as a purely technical exercise, without asking who could be harmed by the design.

The refinement: I revisited that specific proposal and changed seven concrete elements of it, driven by one question: how do we scale to billions of records while protecting the privacy of living individuals and the sanctity of temple ordinance data?

Seven specific changes to the Heritage Vault 2.0 proposal and the ethical reasoning behind each.
Area of ProposalOriginal DesignRefined DesignEthical Reason
User access tiers (Sec. 1)Single permission model for all usersThree tiers: church-member accounts with ordinance access; genealogist accounts with API/bulk access; researcher accounts scoped to aggregated historical dataLeast privilege applied to end users, not only administrators. Restricts sacred records to those with a legitimate claim.
ZFS datasets (Sec. 3)One pool for all genealogical dataSeparate datasets for public records, document images, and restricted ordinance recordsAn administrator managing public data should never accidentally reach sacred records.
Encryption scope (Sec. 2)Volume-level LUKS onlyLUKS plus file-level encryption for ordinance recordsIf storage media is physically compromised, the most sensitive records remain unreadable.
Load balancer routing (Sec. 1)HAProxy across one backend poolDedicated backend pools for API/bulk traffic, separate from casual usersFairness: bulk exports should not degrade response times for a member searching a family name.
CDN justification (Sec. 1)CDN for latency onlyCDN justified on accessibility grounds — serving low-bandwidth regionsEquitable access to family history is a matter of fairness, not only performance.
Access auditing (Sec. 2)AIDE on system filesAIDE plus access auditing on ordinance datasets, including administrator accessBroad access to sacred data requires accountability for the people trusted with it.
Implementation order (Sec. 5)Four equal-priority phasesSecurity foundation prioritized as Phase 1Stewardship means protecting people first. A highly available system that leaks records is a failure.
What this refinement taught me

The original proposal was technically competent but ethically silent — it optimized for uptime without asking who could be harmed. Changing seven specific elements of one specific proposal forced me to apply Stewardship and Integrity to the document itself. Ethics is not a section you add at the end of a proposal; it is a question you ask at the start of every design decision.

Usability Testing Summary

What Testing Revealed

  • Navigation: Testers could not tell where they were in a long page; no sticky navigation existed.
  • Scannability: Dense paragraph blocks meant recruiters could not extract my identity in 60 seconds.
  • Accessibility: Insufficient contrast on small accent text, no visible keyboard focus indicators.
  • Responsiveness: The comparison table overflowed horizontally on a 375px viewport.
  • Functionality: The résumé download link pointed to a missing file.

What Changed

  • Sticky navigation with anchor links to every section, plus scroll-margin-top so headings are not hidden.
  • Mental map restructured into a scannable central node with three connected cards.
  • Consistent Challenge / Solution / Outcome / Reflection blocks for every project.
  • Comparison table wrapped in a scrollable container; Grid with auto-fit collapses cleanly on mobile.
  • Semantic landmarks, one h1, declared language, raised contrast, visible focus rings.

Result: Time to locate contact information fell from 14 seconds to 3; time to reach the Heritage Vault refinement fell from 41 seconds to 6; all three testers could name my three principles after 60 seconds (previously none could); Lighthouse accessibility rose from 78 to 96.
